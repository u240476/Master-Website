Description of Dataset¶

Features are computed from a digitized image of a fine needle aspirate of a breast mass. They describe characteristics of the cell nuclei present in the image. There are 10 real valued features in the dataset: radius, texture, perimeter, area, smoothness, compactness, concavity, concave point, symmetry and fractal dimension. The mean, standard error, and mean of the three largest values were computed for each image, resulting in 30 features and 32 attributes, ID-number and diagnosis.

Justification of Task¶

This is a binary classification task predicting whether a tumor is malignant or benign from 30 nucleus measurements. A decision tree is a reasonable model because it's not black box and is easily interpretable Trees work well with continuous and categorical features, all of the features in the dataset are continuous and they are represented on very different scales and decision trees do not need scaling.

Parameter choice and hypothesis¶

I chose min_samples_leaf as my parameter choice as my default model had 6 leaves with only 1 sample, I hypothesise by increasing this parameter I can improve generalisation for unseen data, reduce tree complexity, improve test performance and reduce the potential overfitting identified in the default model.

Parameter value choice¶

In [7]:
leaf_values = [1, 5, 10]

I chose 1, 5 and 10. The minimum 1 is the default and overfit baseline with six single sample leaves. The value 5 removes the 1 and 3 sample leaves. The maximum 10 still keeps the large leaves. Together they should show how test accuracy changes as overfitting is reduced.

Train/test with 3 metrics¶

In [8]:
from sklearn.metrics import accuracy_score, precision_score, recall_score, f1_score

results = []

for value in leaf_values:

   
    model = DecisionTreeClassifier(
        min_samples_leaf=value,
        random_state=42
    )

    model.fit(X_train, y_train)


    y_pred = model.predict(X_test)

 
    results.append({
        'min_samples_leaf': value,
        'Accuracy': accuracy_score(y_test, y_pred),
        'Precision': precision_score(y_test, y_pred, average='macro'),
        'Recall': recall_score(y_test, y_pred, average='macro'),
        'F1 Score': f1_score(y_test, y_pred, average='macro')
    })


results_df = pd.DataFrame(results)

results_df.round(3)

Increasing min_samples_leaf from 1 to 5 improved accuracy from 0.912 to 0.930 and F1 from 0.907 to 0.923, supporting my hypothesis that removing single-sample leaves reduces overfitting. At 10 accuracy (0.926) and F1 (0.922) were almost unchanged, a difference of about one test sample so performance plateaued rather than clearly falling. Recall kept rising (0.909, 0.915, 0.930) while precision dropped from 0.934 to 0.917 so the larger value traded some precision for recall. These values were too mild to cause underfitting, despite recall improving from 5 to 10 i belive that a size of 5 is the best of the three tested as it had the highest accuracy, precision and F1 while giving a simpler tree than the default.
